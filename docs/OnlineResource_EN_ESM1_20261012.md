**S-Fig 1** Per-dataset quality control (QC) plots after normalization for all 18 bulk transcriptome datasets. (A) Post-normalization expression density: one panel per dataset and one density curve per sample, colored by group (Control blue / Case red / Other gray); within each dataset, the sample curves were highly overlapping in shape and distribution, indicating no systematic expression shift after normalization. (B) Post-normalization principal component analysis (PCA): each panel used the top 5,000 genes by variance; points represent samples, colored by group; panel titles annotate the variance explained by PC1/PC2. The 18 datasets are ordered along four tracks: OA training (GSE55235, GSE55457, GSE12021, GSE51588, GSE114007), OA validation (GSE169077, GSE168505, GSE57218, GSE206848), OA blood (GSE48556), and skeletal muscle (GSE1428, GSE25941, GSE111016, GSE111010, GSE111006, GSE136344, GSE117525, GSE144304). This figure is a within-dataset, post-normalization QC view for inspecting normalization performance and obvious outlier samples in each dataset; the before-versus-after comparison of cross-dataset batch correction is provided by main Fig 2a (merged OA training matrix); the two are complementary and non-redundant.

**S-Fig 2** Complete overview of per-cohort differential-expression volcano plots for the OA training tier. Shown are the case–control differential-expression volcano plots for each of all 5 OA training cohorts—(a) GSE55235, (b) GSE55457, (c) GSE51588, (d) GSE12021, (e) GSE114007; main Fig 2b displays only the representative cohort GSE55235, whereas this figure provides the complete per-cohort overview. Differential-analysis software and sample-handling conventions are as in the main Methods: microarray cohorts (GSE55235/GSE55457/GSE51588/GSE12021) were analyzed with limma and the RNA-seq cohort (GSE114007) with DESeq2 for case–control differential analysis; for GSE51588, within-donor correlation of dual-site samples from the same donors was handled with duplicateCorrelation; GSE12021 was restricted to the GPL96 platform. Per-cohort significant genes (adj.P<0.05 and |log2FC|≥0.585, approximately 1.5-fold) numbered 445–3,423 per cohort (descriptive statistics); points are colored by dysregulation direction and significance (upregulated/downregulated/not significant). The input to RRA cross-cohort integration consisted of per-cohort genome-wide two-sided lists ranked by P value and split by direction; integration yielded 511 integrated significant genes (adj.P<0.05), which entered the construction of the dual-track shared candidate set (leave-one-dataset-out and donor-overlap–corrected sensitivity analyses of the integrated set are given in Tables S19 and S20). The |log2FC|>1 in main Fig 2b is a display and annotation threshold for the representative cohort and does not alter the analysis inclusion conventions above. This figure is a per-cohort supplement to main Fig 2b and supports the main-text statement that "per-cohort differential analysis of the OA training tier yielded 445–3,423 significant genes per cohort."

**S-Fig 3** Dot-plot overview of all significant terms from track-stratified functional enrichment. Shown are all 35 FDR<0.05 significant terms (GO-BP 4, Reactome 28, KEGG 3) from over-representation analysis (ORA, clusterProfiler) across three tracks (concordant track, OA-up/muscle-down mirror track, OA-down/muscle-up mirror track) × three databases (GO-BP, Reactome, KEGG), arranged in full by track × database facets; this is a single-page overview of the same significant set as main Fig 2e/2f. **S-Fig 4** Mirror-direction concordance comparison of the 10 biomarkers in the muscle-proxy exploratory tier. Shown is the comparison of the expression directions of the 10 biomarkers (NELL1, STEAP1, GADD45A, FBLN5, HEMK1, GDE1, RNF14, BNIP3, EGFR, CBR3) in muscle-proxy cohorts (frailty-proxy GSE117525/GSE144304; aging-proxy GSE136344) against the directions expected from the discovery-tier dual tracks (including mirror-track expectations). Per the pre-specified tiered validation framework, the muscle-proxy exploratory tier had no performance threshold and served for direction and lead reporting only; directional concordance was accepted when at least 2 cohorts agreed. The overall directional concordance rate was 9/20 (45%); summarized per gene, 7/10 biomarkers matched track expectations in at least one proxy cohort, among which GDE1 and BNIP3 were directionally concordant in all available cohorts (strict criterion 2/10); NELL1, STEAP1, and RNF14 received no directional support; signals were concentrated in the OA-down/muscle-up mirror track (4/6, 66.7%), compatible with the direction of the FoxO atrophy axis. GSE136344 had an empty intersection with the biomarkers (0/0) and contributed no directional statistics. This tier constitutes frailty/aging proxy-phenotype evidence and does not constitute validation at the level of confirmed sarcopenia; performance claims of AUC greater than 0.7 apply only to the cartilage primary validation layer.

**S-Fig 5** Group-comparison boxplot panels for the immune-infiltration exploratory tiers (three methods × three exploratory tiers, 9 panels in total). Muscle-tier results are stated as "not detected at the current sample size"; aging proxy tier results must not be written as sarcopenia evidence; all results in this section are exploratory.

**S-Fig 8** Upstream TF–miRNA regulatory network of the 10 biomarkers. ## D.2 Source literature

**OA side (three publications, 13 genes after deduplication):**

1. **Lin et al. 2025 (Current Science)**: Druggable-genome-wide summary-data-based MR (SMR) × 7 OA subtypes and 4 OA-related traits, with stepwise stratified validation by colocalization and two-sample MR/pQTL; **6 tier 3 targets**: MAPK3, CSK, IFNGR2, MVD, FES, ITGA2.
2. **Zou et al. 2024 (Biomolecules; PMC10967895)**: Plasma proteome MR × knee/hip OA (12 FDR-corrected candidate proteins, tier-stratified). **Tier 1**: MAPK3 (knee OA, OR=0.855), GZMK (knee OA, OR=1.278), ITIH1 (hip OA, OR=0.847); **tier 2**: LGALS3 (knee OA OR=1.120; SMR validation OR=1.638, concordant direction; coloc PPH4=0.828).
3. **Lu et al. (PLoS One; PMC11813149; 1,553 circulating proteins × 5 OA traits)**: After Bonferroni correction, Steiger filtering, colocalization (PPH4>0.8), and phenome scanning, the authors finalized 4 causal proteins: DNAJB12, USP8 (knee OA), IL12B (spinal OA), RGMB (thumb OA).

**Sarcopenia side (one publication, 6 genes):**

4. **Yin et al. 2024 (J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle; doi:10.1002/jcsm.13479)**: Systematic druggable-genome MR (blood/skeletal-muscle eQTL and pQTL × sarcopenia-related traits: appendicular lean mass, grip strength, walking pace); among 17 MR candidates, **6 were confirmed by Bayesian colocalization (PPH4>0.9)**: MAP3K3, MFGE8, COL15A1, HP, HLA-DRA, AURKA.

## D.4 Control conclusion

The 10 biomarkers of this study (**GADD45A, EGFR, NELL1, STEAP1, FBLN5, RNF14, HEMK1, GDE1, CBR3, BNIP3**) have **no overlap** with either the OA-side 13-gene list (MAPK3, CSK, IFNGR2, MVD, FES, ITGA2, GZMK, LGALS3, ITIH1, DNAJB12, USP8, IL12B, RGMB) or the sarcopenia-side 6-gene list (MAP3K3, MFGE8, COL15A1, HP, HLA-DRA, AURKA) (0/10; overlap analysis record: `results/moduleH/moduleH_known_causal_overlap.csv`; Table S8).

**Interpretation (as stated in the main text):** The candidate-generation logic of this study is "shared across both diseases plus directionality", which is orthogonal to the "single-disease causality" logic of single-disease studies; non-overlap is therefore expected. The biomarkers of this study constitute a complementary **novel candidate layer** rather than a rediscovery of existing targets.

**Usage limitations:** This list was compiled according to the tier 1/2 targets or author-named core targets of each source publication and is not an exhaustive enumeration; other candidates reported by OA MR/TWAS studies (e.g. SMAD3, COL2A1, HHIP, MGP) were not included in the comparison, and the overlap conclusion holds only within the scope of this list.
